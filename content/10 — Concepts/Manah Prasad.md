# Manah Prasad

## What it is

Manah prasad is the gladdening of the mind: a settled, cheerful, clear state from which the rest of the day is met. In this lineage it is taught first. [[Swami Niranjanananda Saraswati]] places it at the head of the yamas and niyamas of the yogic lifestyle, the simple disciplines that come before the great virtues in [[Patanjali]]'s eight limbs.[^1]

It is a practice. A mind does not stay glad by accident, and the lineage treats cheerfulness as something cultivated, the way a field is prepared before anything is sown.

## Sanskrit and etymology

*Manas* (मनस्) is the mind in its thinking, feeling and wanting aspect. See [[Manas]]. *Prasāda* (प्रसाद) means clearness, calm, brightness and grace. The same word names the food offered in a temple and then shared, the gift that comes back blessed. Manah prasad is the mind made clear and bright, the way water clears when it is left to settle.

## Where it appears in the canon

The *Bhagavad Gita* lists the austerities of the mind, and puts manah prasad first, ahead of gentleness, silence, self-restraint and purity of feeling.[^2]

Earlier in the *Gita*, Krishna says that a person who moves among the objects of the senses without attachment or aversion attains *prasada*, and that in that clarity all sorrows come to an end.[^3]

The *Yoga Sutras* use the same root for the mind made clear, *citta-prasādana*, and say it comes from friendliness towards the happy, compassion for the suffering, gladness at the good and even-mindedness towards the harmful.[^4]

## What is contested

**Cause or result.** One reading treats a glad mind as the fruit of long practice. The lineage reading puts it at the start: offer the mind some happiness first, and the deeper practices become possible.

**Happiness or clarity.** *Prasada* can be translated as cheerfulness or as clarity. The two are close, and the difference matters in practice. Forced cheerfulness over real distress is a mask. The lineage's sense is closer to a clear, untroubled mind that can then be glad.

## Why it matters

Most householders meet their day from a tight mind: a child's fever, a parent's call, a difficult meeting, all before nine. Manah prasad changes the posture the day is met from. The duties stay the same. The mind that meets them is steadier. The [[Pancha Kosha]] page explains why the lineage starts here: the body and the breath are both shaped by the mind that sits above them.
