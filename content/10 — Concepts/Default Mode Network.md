# Default Mode Network

## What it is

The default mode network is a set of brain regions that become more active when a person is not focused on a task: daydreaming, remembering, planning, replaying a conversation, worrying about tomorrow. Its activity tends to drop when attention turns to something outside, such as a problem to solve. It is closely linked to self-referential thought, the running story a mind tells about itself.

The core regions are the medial prefrontal cortex, the posterior cingulate cortex and precuneus, and parts of the parietal lobe near the angular gyrus.

## Origin of the term

Marcus Raichle and colleagues described a "default mode of brain function" in 2001, from imaging studies that kept finding the same regions active at rest and quiet during tasks.[^1] The name stuck.

## What the research shows

A large experience-sampling study by Matthew Killingsworth and Daniel Gilbert found that people's minds were wandering in nearly half of their waking moments, and that they were less happy when their minds wandered than when they did not.[^2] The paper's title, "A wandering mind is an unhappy mind", became a common summary.

Judson Brewer and colleagues found that experienced meditators showed less activity in the main default mode regions during meditation than beginners did, across several styles of practice.[^3] Studies of [[Yoga Nidra|yoga nidra]] practitioners have reported changes in the same network.

## Where it meets the tradition

The yogic tradition described the same experience in its own language long before brain imaging. The *Yoga Sutras* call the movements of the mind *vrittis* and define yoga as their stilling. See [[Chitta]]. [[Pratyahara]], the turning of the senses inward, and the practice of witnessing thoughts without following them, are the tradition's methods for working with what neuroscience now calls default mode activity.

## What is contested

**The network has a job.** The same regions support memory, imagination, planning and understanding other people. A mind with no default activity would be impaired. The problem the research points to is getting stuck in repetitive, self-critical loops, and the inability to step out of them at will.

**Reading too much into scans.** Brain imaging shows patterns of activity. Linking a pattern to a specific experience, such as peace or rumination, is an inference. Many meditation studies are small, and findings vary.

## Why it matters

Many people, women in their forties especially, describe a mind they cannot switch off. The default mode network gives that experience a name in the language of science, and the research suggests that trained attention can change how strongly the mind is pulled into it. The tradition's practices offer the training.
