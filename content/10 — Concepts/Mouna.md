# Mouna

## What it is

Mouna is silence kept on purpose. The tradition counts it among the disciplines of the mind, because a silence that is chosen lets the mind settle. A silence forced on a person by fear works the other way, and the practice asks a person to learn the difference.

## Sanskrit and etymology

*Mauna* (मौन) comes from *muni*, a sage, one who keeps silent, from the root *man*, to think. It is the silence of the one who reflects. In everyday Hindi a vow of silence is a *maun vrat*.

## Where it appears in the canon

**Bhagavad Gita 17.16** lists the austerities of the mind: serenity of mind (*manaḥ-prasāda*), gentleness, silence (*mauna*), self-restraint and purity of feeling.[^1] The verse before it deals with speech. By placing mauna with the mind, the Gita treats silence as an inner condition as much as a closed mouth.

**In the [[Bihar School of Yoga]]**, [[Swami Satyananda Saraswati|Swami Satyananda]] taught *antar mouna*, inner silence, as a seated meditation in stages. Attention moves from the senses, to the thoughts that arise by themselves, to thoughts created and let go on purpose, and finally towards the space between thoughts.[^2] It is closely tied to [[Pratyahara]], the withdrawal of the senses.

## What is contested

**Where restraint ends and suppression begins.** Both Indian and Japanese manners prize restraint in speech, and the line between holding back with care and holding back out of fear is not always clear, even to the person doing it. Psychology has studied the second. In a widely cited study, people who habitually suppressed what they felt reported lower wellbeing than people who reappraised the situation.[^3] The tradition's own test is the effect on the mind. Chosen silence leaves it quieter. Swallowed words leave it louder.

## Why it matters

**It is available to anyone, every day.** A few minutes of chosen silence before the household wakes needs no equipment and no teacher, though antar mouna is best learned from one.

**It lets a person hear what they actually think.** Much of a day is spent answering. Mouna is the time in which the answers stop and the questions can be heard.

**It sharpens the difference between two silences.** A person who learns what chosen silence feels like begins to notice the other kind in their own life.
