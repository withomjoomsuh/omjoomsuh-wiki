# Karma

## What it is

Karma is action and its consequence, held together in one word. In the Indian traditions every deliberate act leaves a trace in the one who does it, and that trace shapes what comes back. In the classical account the process needs no judge.

## Sanskrit and etymology

*Karma* (कर्म) comes from the root *kṛ*, to do, to make. It means the deed and also the residue a deed leaves, which is why the same word serves for a day's work and for a theory of moral cause and effect. How to act so that action binds less is the subject of [[Karma Yoga]].

## Where it appears in the canon

**Bhagavad Gita 4.17** says that the way of action is hard to understand (*gahanā karmaṇo gatiḥ*).[^1] The Gita returns to karma throughout, and its second and third chapters build the discipline of karma yoga on it.

**Yoga Sutras 2.12 to 2.14** describe a store of karma rooted in the [[Kleshas|kleshas]], which ripens as birth, span of life and experience, and brings joy or pain according to whether the acts were helpful or harmful.[^2] **4.7** says that the action of a yogi is neither white nor black, while the action of others is of three kinds.

**Vedanta** sorts karma into three heaps. *Sañcita* is what has accumulated. *Prārabdha* is what is already ripening in this life. *Āgāmi* is what is being made now. Short Advaita texts such as the *Tattva Bodha* set this out.[^3] Only the third is in a person's hands.

**A comparison.** The Code of Hammurabi, from Babylon around 1754 BCE, says that if a man puts out the eye of another man, his own eye shall be put out.[^4] That is a rule a king enforces. Karma, as the tradition describes it, works without anyone enforcing it, through the [[Samskaras|samskaras]] an act lays down in the one who acts.

## What is contested

**Karma used against the suffering.** The doctrine has been used to make poverty and caste look deserved, as if a person's present pain were their own account coming due. Reformers inside the tradition have rejected that use for over a century. This wiki records it because it has happened.

**Karma across lives.** The classical texts assume rebirth. Many modern readers keep the idea of consequence within one life and set rebirth aside. Both readings exist among practitioners.

**Collective karma.** Whether a family or a nation can carry karma together is a modern extension. The classical texts speak mostly of individuals.

## Why it matters

**It gives responsibility without blame.** The past cannot be undone, and the tradition does not ask anyone to punish themselves for it. It asks what is being made now.

**It puts the present act first.** Of the three heaps, only *āgāmi* is open. That is the practical centre of the whole idea.

**It explains why small daily acts count.** If every act leaves a trace, then a daily practice is a daily trace, laid down in the opposite direction.
