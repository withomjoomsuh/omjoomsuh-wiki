# Vama Marga

## What it is

Vama marga is the left-hand path of Tantra. It is one of two broad streams. The right-hand path, *dakshina marga*, keeps to conventional ritual purity and renunciation. The left-hand path takes the things ordinary religion keeps at a distance, the senses, the body, desire and the feminine, and treats them as means of awakening. In this stream the divine feminine, [[Shakti]], is worshipped as the active power of the universe.

## Sanskrit and etymology

*Vāma* (वाम) means left. It also means beautiful, and it can mean woman. *Mārga* means path. The name holds all three senses at once: the left path, the beautiful path, the path of the feminine. *Dakṣiṇa* means right, and also skilful or conventional.

## Where it appears in the canon

The *Kularnava Tantra* is one of the main texts of the Kaula schools, where left-hand practice is set out. It describes the five elements of ritual, the *panchamakara*, and it also gives each an inner, yogic meaning.[^1]

[[Swami Satyananda Saraswati]] discusses both paths in *Kundalini Tantra*. He presents vama marga as a path open to householders, in which ordinary life, including married life, is joined with yogic practice under guidance, aimed at the awakening of [[Kundalini|kundalini]].[^2]

The wiki's [[Samskaras]], [[Shadripus]] and [[Sadhana]] pages draw on the tantric texts' detailed treatment of how impressions are stored in the body and worked with.

## What is contested

**Literal or inner.** The five elements of left-hand ritual can be read literally or as symbols of inner processes. The texts themselves give both readings, and the tradition has argued over them for centuries. Most lineage teachers today, including this one, emphasise the inner reading and the need for a qualified guide.

**Reputation.** Vama marga has been misunderstood both by outsiders, who read it as licence, and by some who practised it as licence. Its classical texts are strict about discipline and about who is ready.

## Why it matters

For a householder, the significance of vama marga is its starting point. It does not ask a person to leave the world before the spiritual life can begin. It treats family, body and desire as the material of practice, held inside discipline. That is close to how this lineage teaches the householder path, and close to the place [[Kama]] holds among the [[Purusharthas]].
