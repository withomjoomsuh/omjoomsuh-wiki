# Shakti

## What it is

Shakti is power understood as a principle: the capacity by which anything at all is able to happen. In the traditions that use the word technically, it names the active, generative side of reality, held as feminine, and set against a witnessing side held as masculine and still.

The formulation most often quoted is that consciousness without its power is inert. Shiva is awareness. Shakti is what awareness can do. The pairing is not two beings in a relationship so much as one reality described from two sides, and the tradition insists the two are undivided at the root.

For this work the term matters in three places at once. It is the philosophical name for the dynamic principle. It is the goddess addressed by name in the [[32 Names of Durga]] chanted in the morning practice. And it is the energy the subtle-body literature describes lying dormant at the base of the spine, which is where the word most often enters a modern practitioner's vocabulary.

## Sanskrit and etymology

*Śakti* (शक्ति) is a feminine noun from the verbal root *śak*, meaning to be able, to be capable, to have potential.[^1] Monier-Williams gives the range as energy, ability, strength, effort, power, might, capability.

The etymology is worth dwelling on because it is unusually plain. The word does not mean force or dominance. It means *ableness*. Everything else the term carries is built on that ordinary sense of being able to do a thing, raised to a cosmological principle.

The root goes back further, to a reconstructed Indo-European form carrying the sense of achieving or being capable.[^1]

## Where it appears in the canon

**The *Devi Mahatmya*** is the foundational text. It occupies chapters 81 to 93 of the *Markandeya Purana*, runs to seven hundred verses across thirteen chapters, and is therefore also known as the Durga Saptashati, the seven hundred verses to Durga.[^2] Its dating is debated and generally placed between 400 and 600 CE; Wendy Doniger O'Flaherty dates the text itself to around 550 CE and the rest of the *Markandeya Purana* to around 250 CE, which makes the *Devi Mahatmya* a later insertion into an older work.[^2]

Its importance is structural rather than only devotional. It is the first surviving text to treat the Goddess as ultimate reality in her own right, rather than as the consort or power of a male deity. Everything that follows in Shakta literature is downstream of that move.

**The [[32 Names of Durga]]**, chanted in the morning sadhana, come from this body of literature.

**In the subtle-body and tantric material** carried by the [[Bihar School of Yoga]], shakti appears as kundalini, described as coiled and dormant at mooladhara and, when roused, ascending the sushumna through the [[Chakras|chakras]] toward sahasrara. The ascent is conventionally described as shakti travelling to meet shiva.

**Adi Shankaracharya** is traditionally credited with the *Saundarya Lahari*, a hymn to the Goddess, which sits interestingly beside his Advaita commentaries and is one reason the relationship between Vedanta and Shakta devotion is less tidy than summaries suggest.

## The structural parallel with Samkhya, and where it inverts

This is the observation most worth carrying away, and it is rarely stated plainly.

In [[Samkhya]], *Prakriti* is active, generative, and grammatically feminine. *Purusha* is inert, witnessing, and masculine. In Shakta thought, Shakti is active, generative and feminine, while Shiva is the still witness. The architecture is identical.

The valuation is opposite. Samkhya's goal is *kaivalya*, the isolation of the witness from nature, achieved by discriminating one from the other. Shakta practice moves the other way, toward union with the active principle rather than separation from it, and treats the manifest world as the Goddess rather than as what must be seen through.

Two systems, the same skeleton, contrary conclusions. This wiki draws on both, and a reader should know that the [[Samkhya]] page and this one are not describing one continuous tradition.

## What is contested

**The origins of Goddess worship are genuinely unsettled.** A substantial body of scholarship holds that Shakta traditions absorbed older regional and village goddess cults that were not Vedic, and the *Devi Mahatmya* represents the point at which that material was brought fully inside the Sanskritic canon. Others read more continuity from Vedic sources. The evidence does not settle it.

**Philosophical Shakti and devotional Shakti are not obviously the same thing.** One is an abstract principle of capability, the other is a Goddess with names, weapons and a battlefield. The traditions treat them as identical. Whether that identification is argued for or assumed varies by text.

**Vama marga, the left-hand path**, is part of this territory and is the part most often either sensationalised or silently omitted. This wiki notes its existence and does not teach it.

**Modern usage has thinned the word considerably.** Shakti in contemporary wellness writing frequently means little more than feminine energy as a mood. The classical term is a metaphysical category, and the drift is worth resisting in anything published here.

## Why it matters

**It is the philosophical basis for who this work is for.** A tradition in which the generative principle is feminine, and in which the highest reality is addressed as a woman, is not making a concession when it centres women. It is being consistent. That is a stronger foundation than any contemporary framing available.

**It is what the morning chant is actually doing.** The third of the three mantras is addressed to the Goddess by thirty-two names. A practitioner who does not know what shakti means is chanting something whose object is unnamed for them.

**It names the thing the household runs on.** The classical formulation, that awareness without its power accomplishes nothing, describes with some precision the position of a woman holding a house together: the capability through which everything in that house actually happens, and the one most likely to go unnamed.

**It keeps the subtle-body language honest.** Kundalini is a shakti concept, and treating it as a standalone technique detached from this frame is how the term became a marketing word. The [[Bihar School of Yoga]] teaches it inside its own tradition, slowly, and this wiki follows that.
