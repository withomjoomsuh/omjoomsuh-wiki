# Chitta

## What it is

Chitta is the mind-stuff in which every experience leaves its trace. In the yoga tradition it is both the field the mind moves in and the store where impressions are kept, long after the experience that made them has been forgotten. Patanjali's definition of yoga is a statement about chitta: yoga is the stilling of its movements.

## Sanskrit and etymology

*Citta* (चित्त) comes from the root *cit*, to perceive, to be aware. The same root gives *cit*, consciousness, and *cetanā*, awareness. Chitta is what has been perceived and is still held.

In Vedanta, chitta is one of the four functions of the *antaḥkaraṇa*, the inner instrument, beside [[Manas]], [[Buddhi]] and [[Ahamkara]]. In that scheme manas receives and wavers, buddhi decides, ahamkara claims, and chitta remembers.[^1]

## Where it appears in the canon

**Yoga Sutras 1.2** defines yoga as *citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*, the stilling of the movements of chitta. **1.5 to 1.11** list five kinds of movement: right knowing, wrong knowing, imagination, sleep and memory.[^2]

**The store.** The commentarial tradition, beginning with Vyasa, describes chitta as the seat of the [[Samskaras|samskaras]] and the *vāsanās*, the impressions and the lingering scent they leave. In that picture an experience that is never fully lived through does not disappear. It sinks, and it shapes later reactions from below.

**In the [[Bihar School of Yoga]]**, [[Swami Satyananda Saraswati|Swami Satyananda]] described the deeper layers of chitta as the place [[Yoga Nidra]] reaches. In the state between waking and sleep, impressions held there can rise into awareness and be met without being acted out.[^3]

## What is contested

**Whether chitta is simply "mind".** Translations vary: mind, mind-stuff, consciousness, the field of consciousness. [[Samkhya]] does not use chitta as a separate category. Its map has manas, buddhi and ahamkara, and yoga uses chitta as the name for the three working together. This wiki follows the yoga usage.

**Whether chitta is the unconscious.** The picture of a store of impressions resembles the unconscious of Freud and Jung, and writers have drawn the comparison for a century. The traditions were built for different ends, and the resemblance is partial.

## Why it matters

**It explains why some things will not stay buried.** If an impression sinks into chitta when it is not lived through, then the old grief that returns at a wedding, or with a smell, has an explanation. Chitta is doing what chitta does.

**It explains why practice is daily.** The movements of chitta settle a little each time the mind is held still. Nothing in the tradition promises that one sitting empties the store.

**It is the ground the rest of the vocabulary stands on.** [[Samskaras]], [[Kleshas]] and the vrittis all describe what happens in chitta.
