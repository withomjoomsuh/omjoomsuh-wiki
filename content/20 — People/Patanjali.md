# Patanjali

## Who he was

Patanjali is the name attached to the *[[Yoga Sutras]]*, the short Sanskrit text that became the classical statement of yoga as a discipline of the mind. Almost nothing is known of him as a person. There is no reliable biography, no place of birth and no securely dated life. What survives is the text, and a tradition of stories that grew up around its author.

The best-known story makes him an incarnation of Adishesha, the serpent on which Vishnu rests, who fell as a small figure into the folded palms of a woman named Gonika. The name is often read from that story: *pata*, fallen, and *anjali*, folded hands. A traditional invocation, still chanted before yoga classes, honours him as the one who removed the impurities of the mind through yoga, of speech through grammar, and of the body through medicine.

## When he lived

Dates for the *Yoga Sutras* range across seven centuries, from the second century BCE to the fifth century CE. The older date rests on the tradition that the author of the *Yoga Sutras* is the same Patanjali who wrote the *Mahabhashya*, the great commentary on Panini's grammar, around the second century BCE.

Much current scholarship places the *Yoga Sutras* later. Philipp Maas has argued that the sutras and the earliest commentary, attributed to Vyasa, were composed together as a single work around 325 to 425 CE, and that the author of that work may have been one person.[^1] The two-Patanjali question stays open.

## What he did

Meditation, breath control and the disciplines of restraint were practised long before him, in the Upanishads, in the Buddhist and Jain traditions and in the epics. His contribution was to gather them into a compact and ordered system. The *Yoga Sutras* define yoga in its second sentence as the stilling of the movements of [[Chitta|chitta]], name the [[Kleshas|kleshas]] that cloud the mind, set out the eight limbs of practice, and end with *kaivalya*, the freedom of the seer.[^2]

## Patanjali in this lineage

The [[Bihar School of Yoga]] treats Patanjali's eight limbs as the "university syllabus" of yoga: the great virtues and disciplines to be perfected over a life. [[Swami Satyananda Saraswati]] wrote a full commentary on the text, *Four Chapters on Freedom*, which remains the lineage's main reference.[^3] [[Swami Niranjanananda Saraswati]] adds a simpler set of lifestyle yamas and niyamas that come first, beginning with [[Manah Prasad|manah prasad]], happiness of mind.

## What is contested

**One Patanjali or two.** The tradition joins the grammarian and the yogi. Many modern scholars separate them, and some go further and doubt that the *Yoga Sutras* had a single author at all.

**How much is his.** The *Yoga Sutras* draw on [[Samkhya]] philosophy and share vocabulary with Buddhist texts on meditation. Scholars argue over how much is original synthesis and how much is careful compilation.[^4]
