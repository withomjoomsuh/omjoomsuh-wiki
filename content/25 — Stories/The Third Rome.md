# The Third Rome

*Three stories of one Russian family, told by the neighbour downstairs.*

These stories are fiction. The teachings they touch come from the tradition.

The tradition's longest story about duty is the *Mahabharata*, and it does not end cleanly. Its own verdict is that the truth of dharma is hidden in a cave. These three stories borrow that honesty. They are set in Kaliningrad, the piece of Russia that sits alone on the Baltic between Poland and Lithuania, and they follow one family from the winter the Soviet Union ended to an Easter night in 2027 that has not happened.

A mother makes rye vodka and sells her first bottle to a Ukrainian. A father strays for twenty years and comes back at an Easter service. Two daughters grow up into two different Russias. The younger, Nina, loves the West and, in time, a Polish woman. The elder, Katya, studies law in America, comes home and goes into the service of the state, and tries to keep all four of her duties at once. An old literature teacher who lives below them tells it, in the manner of Dostoevsky, with Dostoevsky's lines in the mouths of the people.

## The three parts

- [[Wind of Change]]. Part I. 1991 to 1999. A school concert as the Soviet flag comes down, rye traded for sugar, and a promise about NATO that one daughter never forgets. About 24 minutes.
- [[Not One Inch]]. Part II. 2003 to 2016. A year in Washington, a marriage close to power, a husband who cannot answer at Easter, and a daughter who tells the truth at the table in the spring of Crimea. About 27 minutes.
- [[A Fourth There Shall Not Be]]. Part III. 2022 to an imagined 2027, when the war reaches the city. A tile carried back to Germany, a drawer locked in Moscow, and a paper on a windowsill. About 26 minutes.

Read them in order. Each part ends with an epilogue that names the patterns underneath the story and links each one to the pages where the tradition speaks to it. The last epilogue reads Katya through the [[Four Dharmas]] and the four aims of a human life, and ties the whole story back to [[Dharma]].

## What the record shows

The characters hold the Russian reading of the years after 1991 with full conviction. That reading is part of who they are. The record itself is mixed, and a reader should know where.

- In February 1990, during talks on German unification, US Secretary of State James Baker told Gorbachev three times that NATO's jurisdiction would not move "one inch" east. Declassified records published by the National Security Archive in 2017 show similar assurances from other Western leaders. None of it was written into a treaty, and Gorbachev later said that NATO expansion as such was not discussed.
- In December 1991 Yeltsin wrote to NATO raising Russian membership as a long-term goal. In 2000 the new Russian president asked NATO's Secretary General when Russia would be invited to join, and was told that countries apply.
- Poland, Hungary and the Czech Republic joined NATO in 1999, and Lithuania and six others in 2004. At Bucharest in 2008 NATO declared that Ukraine and Georgia would one day become members.
- Russia calls the 2014 Maidan a Western-backed coup. Ukraine and the West call it a popular revolution. In December 2022 Angela Merkel said the Minsk agreements were an attempt to give Ukraine time. In the same interview she called them an attempt to prevent war.
- In November 2023 Russia's Supreme Court declared "the international LGBT movement" extremist.
- Everything after 2025 in Part III is invented. Nothing on the record shows a ground war in the Kaliningrad region.

## The title

Early in the sixteenth century the monk Philotheus of Pskov wrote to the grand prince of Moscow: two Romes have fallen, the third stands, and a fourth there shall not be. One reading is a boast, that Moscow is the last guardian of the faith. Another is a warning, that if the third falls there is nothing after it. The stories hold both.
